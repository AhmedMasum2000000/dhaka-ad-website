01

Choose the format for its job

A newspaper ad needs a clear booking specification. A retail display needs to suit the shop. Packaging has to work around the product and its handling needs. Start with the intended use instead of copying a format from a previous campaign. That makes the brief more useful to both the designer and the production team.

  • Newspaper advertising and media booking.
  • Printing and packaging production.
  • Point-of-sale and retail display material.
  • Campaign collateral that matches your wider creative.

02

Proof the boring details carefully

Check spelling, numbers, dates, addresses and contact details before production. Review the artwork at the intended size, not only enlarged on a screen. Paper, material, finishing and colour expectations should be described clearly. Where a physical sample or proof is required, include it in the agreed scope and schedule.

03

Understand the booking and delivery terms

Confirm publication dates, positions, sizes and artwork deadlines for media bookings. For print, agree the quantity, material, dimensions, finishing and delivery. Review a clear production specification before approving the work.

04

Keep one brand across the campaign

The shop display, newspaper ad and outdoor creative do not need identical layouts. They do need a recognisable message and consistent details. Keep one approved reference for the offer and contact information. It saves the awkward discovery that three suppliers printed three slightly different versions of the same launch.

05

A simpler way to start

Tell us who you want to reach, where and when. Bring any existing artwork. We will discuss suitable locations, creative requirements and reporting, then confirm the scope in a written proposal you can review.
