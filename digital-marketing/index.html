01

Choose the goal before the platform

Start with the people you need to reach and the response you want. Then compare the role of search, social and your website. Give each channel a clear job, and keep the message consistent from the first ad to the page a customer visits.

  • Social media management and content planning.
  • Facebook, Instagram, Google and YouTube advertising.
  • SEO, landing-page and website work.
  • Video and creative assets for the agreed channels.

02

Make the landing experience match the promise

A good ad can earn a click. The page has to make that click worthwhile. Show the offer clearly, answer the questions people are likely to have and make the next step easy on a phone. If your team receives leads, agree how quickly they can respond and what information they need.

03

Keep responsibilities clear from the start

Agree who handles planning, creative, account management, the website and reporting. Confirm account ownership and the access each team needs. Reporting should show platform delivery and the business actions that can be checked.

04

Use evidence to improve the next decision

Review the campaign against its agreed goal. A high reach figure and a useful enquiry are different measures. SEO also needs time, useful content and a technically accessible website; a ranking promise is not a substitute for a sound plan. Use the reports to choose what to improve rather than to collect impressive-looking screenshots.

05

A grounded approach to search and AI discovery

Useful original information and accessible pages give search systems something reliable to work with. Google explains that its established SEO fundamentals also apply to generative AI search. A technically sound plan and clear business information matter more than a promise that an answer engine will recommend you.
