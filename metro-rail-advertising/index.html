03

Start with the station and the audience

The busiest-looking station is not automatically the best fit. Think about where customers live, work, study or change their journey. A nearby showroom, an education service and a broad brand launch may need quite different placements. Compare the walking route and the actual advertising surface as well as the station name.

  • Station branding in agreed spaces.
  • Pillar or viaduct placements along selected routes.
  • Train-branding options, subject to the relevant approvals.

04

Match the artwork to the viewing distance

A commuter walking past a panel reads differently from someone seeing a pillar from a vehicle. Keep the main message clear and leave breathing room around the brand. A design can be attractive on a laptop and surprisingly hard to read on a moving journey. Review a placement mock-up before approving production.

05

Leave room for the real production process

Physical branding takes coordination. The surface, artwork, printing, permissions and installation all affect the schedule. Ask what is included in your proposal and when each approval is needed. Treat a proposed start date as confirmed only once the placement and necessary approvals are agreed.

  • A station or route shortlist.
  • Format-specific artwork and production requirements.
  • Installation scope and an agreed photo-report plan.

06

Connect the commute with the next touchpoint

A relevant roadside screen or a digital campaign can carry the same idea beyond the station. Start with one clear message across the mix. Additional channels should have a job, whether that is helping people recognise the brand or giving interested customers somewhere useful to go next.
